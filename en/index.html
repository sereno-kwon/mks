MKSTokorozawa, Japan · Since 1950

A small Japanese factory
that has spent seventy years
on the pedal.

MKS (Mikashima Industrial, 三ヶ島製作所) is a specialist bicycle pedal maker founded in 1950 in Tokorozawa, Saitama, Japan. Not frames, not wheels, not a whole groupset: MKS has focused on the pedal alone, and still makes its pedals in its own factory in Tokorozawa.

MKS pedals are not flashy. Instead they have precisely machined bodies and spindles, hand-adjusted cup-and-cone bearings, and a construction you can still take apart and rebuild decades later. That is why MKS has long been the default choice on classic road and touring bikes, fixed gears and everyday bicycles.

Craft

Pedals built to last.

The pedal is the part of a bicycle that is stepped on most and knocked most often. So MKS makes pedals not as consumables but as parts you service and keep using: open the bearings, repack the grease, replace only what is worn, tighten them up and ride again.

The precision that began with track pedals for keirin racers carries straight through to touring pedals and city flat pedals. It is why Japanese bicycle craftsmen and classic bike builders around the world choose MKS.

MachiningPrecision machining

Bodies and spindles are machined in our own factory. Parts made to tight tolerances stay free of play for years.

BearingsCup-and-cone bearings

The traditional hand-adjusted ball bearing. Take it apart, repack the grease and adjust it again.

KeirinTrust from the track

Experience from making track pedals that pass the strict NJS standard of Japanese keirin goes into every pedal.

EverydayEveryday bikes

Factory · Tokorozawa, Saitama

Tokorozawa, not far from Tokyo. MKS has made nothing but pedals here for more than seventy years.

MKS is a textbook Japanese manufacturer, devoted to a single component for a very long time. New models arrive, but the basic construction barely changes, and an MKS pedal bought long ago can be serviced the same way.

From the era of quill pedals and toe clips to wide flat pedals and quick-release systems, the MKS range has grown with the way bicycles are used. The principle stays the same: pedals that spin well, last long and can be repaired.

Classic Quill

SylvanSylvan

classic bicycles best.
Sylvan.

Sylvan is MKS's signature quill pedal: thin metal cages support the shoe, with the body and spindle running between them in the traditional way. It looks most at home on vintage road bikes, randonneurs and fixed gears.

It comes in three versions by use: Road, Touring and Track. Road and Track are single-sided pedals used with toe clips and straps, while Touring can be ridden on either side, making it easy in regular shoes. All three have serviceable cup-and-cone bearings.

A pedal completed by toe clips

Sylvan Road and Track come into their own with toe clips and straps. The foot sits in the same place on the pedal every time, and you can use both the push and the pull. MKS also makes toe clips to match its pedals.

LambdaLambda

A pedal you step on
with your whole foot.
Comfortable all the way.

Lambda is a double-sided platform pedal that supports the whole sole of the foot. The contact area is wide and gently raised in the middle, so even on long rides in regular shoes without clips, pressure never concentrates on one point. In the US it is also known as the Grip King.

Long-distance touring, bikepacking and the daily commute. It is the first pedal MKS recommends to riders who do not wear clipless shoes.

Quick Release

EZY SystemEZY System

Pedals that come off
and go back on without tools.
EZY.

EZY is MKS's quick-release pedal system. With a dedicated adapter fitted to the crank, the pedal can be pulled off and refitted by hand without tools. To fit it, just push it in until it locks; to remove it, pull back the sleeve.

When folding a bike smaller, loading it into a car or train, or storing it in a narrow hallway or on a wall, the pedals no longer stick out. One pair of adapters also lets you swap between several EZY pedals.

See it at Rubi Workshop →Specification
Step 01

Fit the adapter

Fit the EZY adapter to the crank once, the same way you would fit a normal pedal.

Step 02

Push in to lock

Push the pedal into the adapter and it clicks into place, held firmly while you ride.

Step 03

Pull to release

Pull back the sleeve and the pedal comes off. No tools, no force.

Story

1950 ~ 2026Tokorozawa · Japan

Making one component
well, for a long time.
The MKS story.

In 1950, as Japan's post-war bicycle industry was getting back on its feet, Mikashima Industrial began making pedals in Tokorozawa. Track pedals for Japanese keirin, the Sylvan for classic road, the Lambda for touring, flat pedals and EZY for the city. The history of MKS has moved with the changes in Japanese cycling culture.
